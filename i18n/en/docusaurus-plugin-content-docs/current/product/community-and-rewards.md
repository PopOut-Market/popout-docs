# Community & Rewards

Buying and selling is the marketplace. This page covers the layer around it — the
neighbourhood feed people read when they are not shopping, and the standing and
rewards they build up by taking part.

## Community

Community is a home for neighbourhood life inside the app: a place, separate from
buying and selling, to see what is happening nearby. It occupies the bottom-bar
spot that used to hold Saved (which now lives inside the **Me** tab, under "My
listings").

The screen has a header shaped like the shopping feed's — the neighbourhood name
as a tappable title that opens the same suburb picker, and the notification bell
on the right — over a row of topic filters and a feed.

### The feed

A single vertical list of full-width cards, separated by full-width dividers.
Unlike the shopping feed it is **not** ranked by proximity: posts come from across
the whole served area, **newest first**, and nothing but a post's own time decides
where it sits.

Topic filters behave as radio buttons — exactly one is always chosen, and **All**
is the default.

### The topics

Beyond All, there are five neighbourhood topics.

| Topic | What belongs there |
| --- | --- |
| **Questions & reports** | Questions looking for an answer, and reports of local incidents |
| **Tips & deals** | Anything genuinely useful to someone else — shop discounts, good restaurants, transport deals, small-business promotions |
| **Neighbourhood life** | Personal stories, and posts looking for friends or a group |
| **Wanted** | Every flavour of "looking to buy" |
| **Other** | Anything that fits none of the above |

The boundaries are not always obvious. "Anyone know what that bang was last
night?" could be a report or just neighbourhood life; "the restaurant I went to
today was cheap and generous" could be life or a tip. So posts nobody has
categorised are sorted in the background, and until one is sorted its card shows
no topic label at all.

### The card

Each card is laid out sideways: a text column on the left and an optional square
photo on the right, sized to be seen rather than hinted at. A post with no photo
stretches its text to the full width and leaves no gap.

The card is headed by the post's topic label, on its own line above the title.
Reading what a post *is* before reading its title tells you what you are about to
read. The label appears only while you are on **All**, and only once the post has
been sorted into a topic — a card with no topic to show has no line for it at all,
not a blank one.

Under that comes the title, then a one-line preview ending in an ellipsis when it
runs long. Both are set a step smaller than a shopping card's, so a text-forward
card reads as calm rather than shouty.

A full-width row underneath carries the neighbourhood and how long ago it was
posted on the left, and the like and comment counts on the right. Counts appear
only when there is at least one. There is no view count on the card.

### What you can do

- **Read** — open to anyone, signed in or not.
- **Write, like, reply** — signed-in members only.
- **Polls** — posts that collect votes.
- **Photos in replies**, not just in the original post.
- **Location on a post**, and a **community map** view.
- **Share** — a button in a post's top-right corner sends its title, its
  neighbourhood, and a link. Anyone reading may use it, signed in or not,
  including the author.
- **Report** content for review.

## Reviews after a trade

After two people complete a trade, each can leave the other a short review: a
thumbs up or down, and — when it was not great — a few quick reasons why.

**Only one thing is public: a count of how many people were satisfied with a
person.** Everything else a reviewer says (the verdict, any reasons, any note) is
kept private and used only to keep the marketplace healthy.

That is deliberate. A buyer deciding whether to message a stranger about a
second-hand item wants a simple, honest signal that other people have traded well
with them. A single "how many people were satisfied" count gives that signal
without turning the app into a place where people air complaints in public.
Unhappy feedback is still collected — quietly, as a safety signal rather than a
public scarlet letter.

The review is offered in the moment the sale is recorded, and it is entirely
optional. A happy review is one tap. Marking an item sold always works whether or
not a review follows.

## Profile levels

Every member has one level, shown publicly next to their name wherever it appears.
It is not per suburb, not per category, and not split between buying and selling.

The ladder runs through ten named steps:

1. Newcomer
2. Neighbour
3. Regular
4. Local
5. Veteran
6. Trusted
7. Star
8. Champion
9. Icon
10. Legend

### How points are earned

| What the member does | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Someone likes something they wrote (a post or a reply) | 1 per like |
| A trade partner marks them as satisfied for the first time | 10 |

**Nothing a member does alone earns anything.** Writing a post earns nothing.
Writing a reply earns nothing. Every point requires another person to act — to
like what you wrote, or to finish a trade with you and say they were happy. That
is the whole anti-farming design, and it is why there is no way to type your way
up the ladder.

Three consequences worth stating plainly:

- **Writing earns through what it attracts, never through the act.** Posting
  rubbish to climb earns exactly nothing, because nobody likes rubbish.
- **Each person's like on a given piece of writing pays once, for good.** Like,
  unlike, and like again is still one point. Ten different people liking one post
  is ten points; one person liking it ten times is one.
- **Only a *first* satisfied review from each distinct partner pays.** Trading
  with the same person again earns nothing further. A "not satisfied" review earns
  nothing and never subtracts anything. There is no cap on distinct partners.

Nothing else earns points. Buying, selling, listing, inviting, verifying a suburb,
voting in a poll, and reading all earn zero — coins already reward the trading
side.

A daily ceiling on points from likes exists as an anti-collusion guard. It is
invisible: no screen shows it, nobody is warned about it, and it is set far above
what a genuine member will ever reach. Satisfied trade reviews sit outside it,
because a member cannot manufacture them.

### Points vs. coins from the same review

These are deliberately different things, and the difference matters:

| | Level points | Coins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid to | The person the review is **about** | The person who **writes** the review |
| Which verdicts pay | Satisfied only | Any verdict |
| Cap | None | Stops after three distinct partners |

Two separate rewards for two separate people out of the same event: the reviewer
is paid for taking part, the reviewed earns standing for having traded well.
Neither changes the other, and a member can hold both.

## Coins

Coins reward members for bringing the marketplace to life. The point is to make
the app feel alive and trustworthy: members cash coins out for a real voucher, see
that the app actually delivers, and tell other people about it.

### How coins are earned

| Source | Coins | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joining (welcome bonus) | 5 | Once per account |
| Joining with a friend's invite code | 15 | Once per account |
| A listing reviewed and approved | 10 | 6 approved listings (60) |
| A friend joins with your invite code | 15 | 10 friends (150) |
| Reviewing a trade partner for the first time | 15 | 3 distinct partners (45) |
| Reaching a new profile level | 5–30 | 150 over a full climb |

A member who joins using a friend's code gets both the welcome bonus and the
invited-friend credit — 20 coins to start. The ten-friend limit stops only the
*inviter's* earning: a friend who joins after that still gets their own 15 coins,
so inviting more friends is always worth it for them.

Level coins sit **outside** every cap on this list. The six-listing, ten-friend,
and three-partner caps each count only their own kind of coin.

### Review, then credit

A new listing goes live on the marketplace **immediately**. For the reward it sits
in an "under review" state, with a short plain line telling the member coins will
arrive if it follows the rules. Review is usually within 48 hours; the listing
stays fully live and usable throughout.

Coins are credited **only on approval** — nothing approves on its own. Once six
listings have been approved, a further listing is still reviewed and can still be
found perfectly fine; it simply earns no more coins, and is marked **Closed**
rather than approved. "Approved" always means "this listing paid its 10 coins", so
it never appears on a listing that paid nothing.

Putting a listing on hold for a buyer never costs the reward — a reserved listing
is still a real, live listing and is reviewed like any other.

### Coins are final

A member never sees coins arrive and then vanish. Coins appear only once genuinely
earned, so there is nothing held back and nothing to take back:

- A reviewed listing's coins are not clawed back.
- Deleting the listing does not remove them.
- Invite coins stay even if that friend later deletes their account or is banned.

"Final" means coins are never clawed back over an **outcome**. It does not mean
they outlive the account: unspent coins do not survive account deletion or a ban.
An already-owned voucher is a separate real card and is unaffected.

## Vouchers

The rewards hub is for earning; the vouchers page is for spending and holding.
**No real money is ever involved** — vouchers are bought with coins only.

| Voucher | Price |
| --- | --- |
| $5 Woolworths gift card | 30 coins |
| $10 Woolworths gift card | 50 coins |
| $10 Coles gift card | 50 coins |

Buying is instant: the coins are spent and the voucher appears in the member's
wallet straight away, ready to scan at a till **even with no phone signal**. A
member can buy as many as their balance covers.

Each product card stands on its own for affordability. A card you cannot yet
afford shows a greyed-out price button and a short "N more coins" line giving
exactly how far off that specific card is — so a member with 35 coins sees the $5
card buyable and the $10 card greyed with "15 more coins", and never meets a
silent button.

The wallet is reached from the vouchers page or from a **My gift cards** entry in
the **Me** tab. The rewards hub previews the buyable catalogue; it never shows the
wallet.

Gift cards are named in words only — no retailer logos appear anywhere. Small
print on the page states that PopOut Market is independent and not affiliated with
Woolworths Group or Coles Group, and that Apple is not a sponsor of, or involved
in, any promotion within the app.
